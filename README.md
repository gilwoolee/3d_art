# Pen Art

A worked example of planning a wire sculpture that a robot arm draws in the
air with a 3D pen. You go from a mesh to a print plan that a separate check
has replayed with the whole robot: about ten minutes on a 24-core machine.

![The viewer on the ghost: its picture, the textured model, the frame
(green) and detail (orange), and the robot drawing it](docs/images/viewer_ghost.png)

Everything is simulation. Nothing has been printed, and the pen, the wire and
the arm's limits are assumptions, not measurements.

## What it does

A 3D pen pushes out hot plastic that hardens in a second, so a robot holding
one can draw a wire frame in mid-air, layer on layer: loops around the object
joined by zigzags, as in [WirePrint](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2642918.2647359).
The hard parts are making a frame that stands, drawing it in as few pen-down
strokes as possible, and finding arm motions that never hit the robot, the
plate or the wire already drawn. This repository does each of those as a
separate step you can run and inspect.

## What you need

- Linux x86-64 with git, git-lfs, curl and [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/).
- A machine with many cores: planning uses 24 worker processes (pass
  `--workers` for fewer). The planner runs on the CPU only.
- A mesh. The pipeline assumes one generated by a text- or image-to-3D tool
  such as [Meshy](https://www.meshy.ai): one object, a textured `.glb`, at any
  scale. The ghost in `objs/cute_ghost/` is one. Other meshes may work but are
  not the target.

## Setup

```bash
git clone https://github.com/gilwoolee/3d_art.git && cd 3d_art
scripts/setup_machine.sh
```

This pulls the mesh, makes `.venv`, fetches the Flexiv robot description into
`external/`, builds the robot cell into `runs/cell/` and builds the viewer. If
your shell sets `PYTHONPATH` (for example for ROS), unset it first.

## The steps

Each step is one command that prints one line. The numbers below are what a
fresh clone printed for the ghost.

### 0. Describe the cell: `pen-art cell`

`configs/cell.yaml` is the robot cell: a Flexiv Rizon 4 arm (7 joints), a
256 × 256 mm printer build plate on a 10 cm stand straight in front of it, the
pen, and the build volume (objects are scaled to at most 15 cm).

```
$ .venv/bin/pen-art cell
cell: Rizon4, plate 256 x 256 mm with its top 10 cm up, objects up to 15 cm -> runs/cell/cell.yaml
```

The stand keeps the arm away from its awkward, near-singular poses close to
the table. Setup already ran this; run it again after editing `cell.yaml`.

*Try:* `build_volume_m: 0.08` (see [Changing the setup](#changing-the-setup)),
a longer pen, or the plate further away.

### 1. Mesh to frame: `pen-art frame`

The frame generator slices the mesh into horizontal loops, joins neighbouring
loops with zigzags (a vertical riser, then a slant back down of at most 27°),
fills the outline on the plate with a crossed grid, winds thin parts as
coils, and shrinks the object until the frame needs at most 10 m of wire. It
then adds a little *connector wire* so the frame can be drawn in one stroke
(see [Drawing the frame in one stroke](#drawing-the-frame-in-one-stroke)),
and checks that the frame stands.

```
$ .venv/bin/pen-art frame objs/cute_ghost/cute_ghost.glb --name ghost
frame ghost: 9.9 m of wire, 142 mm tall at most, 1535 wire pieces, stands: yes, fewest trails 1 (was 10 before connector wire)
```

Everything for the ghost goes in `runs/ghost/`. Running `frame` again starts
that folder over. When the frame misses its wire budget, or some odd nodes
could not be joined by connector wire, an extra line says so.

*Try:* `--layer 0.015` (fewer, taller layers, less wire), or `--up z` for a
mesh whose up axis is z. The settings are in `configs/frame.yaml`.

### 2. Add detail: `pen-art detail` (optional)

Detail is a few long strokes over the surface, drawn after the frame and
lying on the frame wire. They follow the creases of the mesh's shape (ridges
and valleys found by slicing it), joined end to end where they meet. Each
stroke keeps one pen orientation; the pen leans toward the side of the object
it draws on, and the arm reaches around the object for the far side.

```
$ .venv/bin/pen-art detail ghost
detail ghost: 2.00 m in 14 strokes, 0.84 m in 31 spares (plan-geometry draws a spare for each stroke the arm cannot draw)
```

This takes about three minutes. This step decides how much detail there is:
the longest drawable lines, up to `--detail` metres (2 m) and `--minutes` of
added print time (15, estimated from the drawing speed). The other lines that
passed its check are kept as *spares*. `--crease-threshold`
(in mm; smaller values add shallower creases) changes what it finds. When it finds nothing drawable it says so and the frame is
planned alone. Detail follows only the *shape*, so it misses
what is only painted in the texture, like the ghost's eyes and mouth. This
step is the place to add an AI agent that picks more artistic detail, for
example from rendered views of the textured mesh.

### 3. Plan the paths: `pen-art plan-geometry`

Geometry first: for every stroke, in build order, the planner finds a
collision-free arm path with the pen at one fixed orientation, checked
against the cell and every wire laid before it. There is no timing yet. When
a stroke starts where the last one ended, it first tries to carry on with the
pen down (*chaining*), and lifts off only when that fails. A detail stroke the
arm cannot draw is dropped, and the longest spare that still fits the detail
budget takes its place, one for one, so the number of strokes never grows. A
frame stroke that cannot be drawn stops the plan.

```
$ .venv/bin/pen-art plan-geometry ghost --frame-only
plan-geometry ghost: GEOMETRY_PLANNED, 1 lift-offs (fewest possible for the frame: 1)
$ .venv/bin/pen-art plan-geometry ghost
  dropping detail the arm cannot draw: tour-020 (no clear approach, drawing or withdrawal, last PEN_WIRE_COLLISION)
  drawing spare detail instead: tour-009
  dropping detail the arm cannot draw: tour-027 (no clear approach, drawing or withdrawal, last PEN_WIRE_COLLISION)
  drawing spare detail instead: tour-006
  dropping detail the arm cannot draw: tour-033 (no clear approach, drawing or withdrawal, last POSTURE_CHANGE)
  drawing spare detail instead: tour-031
plan-geometry ghost: GEOMETRY_PLANNED, 15 lift-offs (fewest possible for the frame: 1), dropped 3 detail strokes the arm cannot draw, drew 3 spares instead
```

The frame alone takes about 16 s and is drawn in one stroke: the only
lift-off is the first approach. With detail it takes about two and a half minutes, and
every detail stroke starts with a lift-off. Each dropped stroke is named with
where the planner last failed on it.

### 4. Time it: `pen-art plan-timing`

The paths are timed once: drawing speed, a cooling hold at each riser top,
and the arm's speed and acceleration limits. This gives the print time. Timing
never adds or removes strokes; when the measured detail comes out over 2 m or
15 added minutes, it says so, and you run `pen-art detail` with less.

```
$ .venv/bin/pen-art plan-timing ghost
plan-timing ghost: FULL_BUILD_PLANNED, 12.7 min of printing, 15 lift-offs (fewest possible for the frame: 1), detail 1.39 m adding 3.1 min
```

The frame alone prints in 9.7 min.

### 5. Verify: `pen-art verify-full-robot`

Planning checked the pen. This separate check replays the saved plan with
the whole robot, interval by interval, against the cell and all the wire
built so far. Only a plan that passes counts.

```
$ .venv/bin/pen-art verify-full-robot ghost
verify-full-robot ghost: FULL_BUILD_VERIFIED (simulation only), 12.7 min of printing, 15 lift-offs (fewest possible for the frame: 1)
```

### 6. Look at it: `pen-art view`

```bash
.venv/bin/pen-art view    # then open http://127.0.0.1:8765
```

Four panels: the object's picture, the textured model, the frame (green) and
detail (orange), and the robot drawing it, with one set of play controls.

The commands work from any folder inside the repository. Stages run in order. Running one before the stage it builds on, after it
failed, or after the frame, the cell or the planning settings changed, stops
with a message naming the stage to run. A failed stage exits with a non-zero
status and points to `runs/NAME/planning.log`.

## Concepts

**Frame and strokes.** The frame is a graph: each piece of wire is an edge,
and the points where pieces meet are nodes. A *stroke* is a run of wire drawn
with the pen down.

**Drawing the frame in one stroke.** Drawing every piece exactly once without
lifting the pen is an *Euler trail* (한붓그리기, a one-stroke drawing), the
problem Euler solved for the
[Seven Bridges of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Bridges_of_K%C3%B6nigsberg)
in 1736. A connected figure can be drawn this way exactly when at most two
nodes have an odd number of pieces; in general it takes half the number of
odd nodes. The ghost's frame has 20 odd nodes, most of them where the base
grid lines end on the outline, so it needs at least 10 strokes. The frame
step pairs those nodes by a minimum-weight matching and joins each pair with
a short connector wire just inside the outline, never on top of other wire.
That leaves two odd nodes, where the drawing starts and ends: one stroke.
Hierholzer's algorithm orders the base on the plate as one closed loop that
ends where the walls begin; the walls are already generated in drawing order,
each zigzag starting where the one below ended, so every piece is drawn after
the wire that holds it up.

**Lift-offs.** Every time the pen lifts, the arm must come back to the same
point to rejoin the wire. It runs open loop, so nothing guarantees it does,
and the new wire must fuse onto wire that may have sagged. That is why
strokes are chained wherever possible, and every plan reports its lift-offs
and their reasons. The frame needs 1; each detail stroke adds one.

**One pen orientation per stroke.** Turning the pen while it deposits would
twist the wire, so each stroke keeps one tilt and one lean direction. Frame
strokes are drawn with the pen upright; detail strokes tilt up to 60°.

**Geometry first.** Paths are planned untimed, then timed once at the end,
instead of timing every candidate inside the search.

**Verification.** Planning checks the pen, approximated by spheres against the
wire; verification replays everything with the whole robot.

## Changing the setup

Frame settings are physical sizes: changing the build volume changes the
object, not the layer height or spacing. A smaller object simply gets fewer
layers and less wire. With `build_volume_m: 0.08` in `configs/cell.yaml`
(then `pen-art cell` and the steps again), a fresh clone printed:

| | 15 cm ghost | 8 cm ghost |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Frame wire | 9.9 m | 3.0 m |
| Fewest frame strokes with connector wire (without) | 1 (10) | 1 (6) |
| Frame print time | 9.7 min | 3.2 min |
| Frame lift-offs | 1 | 1 |
| Detail kept | 1.39 m (+3.1 min) | 1.27 m (+4.2 min) |
| Frame and detail print time | 12.7 min | 7.3 min |
| Lift-offs with detail | 15 | 28 |
| Verdict | `FULL_BUILD_VERIFIED` | `FULL_BUILD_VERIFIED` |

The rest of `configs/frame.yaml` is there to experiment with too. Its
`wireprint`, `graph`, `structure` and `process` sections (frame shape, strand,
material, pen speed and cooling) are copied into the frame, so they take
effect when you run `pen-art frame` again. `timing`, `transit` and `planning`
(arm limits, lifting off and travelling, planner settings) are read when the
paths are planned; after editing them, run `pen-art plan-geometry` again (the
later stages refuse until you do). Detail strokes back away 15 mm instead of
`withdraw_distance_m`, since they lie on the outside of the frame.

## Limitations

These are assumptions, not measurements: the pen's shape and its 0.3 mm
clearance from the wire, the drawing speed and cooling holds, PLA stiffness (a
published 2.5 GPa), the arm's acceleration limits, and that detail laid over
frame wire fuses to it. Planning approximates the pen against wire by spheres
every 10 mm; verification replays the whole robot. Detail strokes are still
separate lift-offs; joining them into fewer strokes is open.
